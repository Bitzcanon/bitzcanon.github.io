Toxic Tide

Toxic Tide is a 2D side-scrolling endless runner that has the player escaping a deadly wall of toxic gas. The player has to make use of their grappling hook to traverse through the obstacle-ridden laboratory, collect gears to upgrade their gear and survive for as long as they can.

This game is the result of a 3-month long school project where the goal was to create a 5-minute gameplay experience that emphasizes on aesthetics and engagement within a limited scope.

3 Designers | 2 Artists
Jan 2025 - Mar 2025 (3 Months)

Roles and Responsibilities

I served as the Technical and Gameplay Designer, responsible for the grappling hook system, camera behavior, audio integration, and data management and serialization for the leaderboard and other game data. I also coordinated the implementation of art assets from our artists.

Later, as part of an audio-focused module, I collaborated with another designer to compose and arrange background music for the Town (2nd level) and Main Menu, and sourced and edited sound effects to better suit the gameplay.

Additionally, I took on the role of Product Manager, delegating tasks, managing deadlines, and ensuring planned gameplay features were feasible within the team's scope and capabilities.

Challenges Faced

Rope Wrapping

The core mechanic of the game was the grappling hook, which was designed to feel fast, responsive, and satisfying to swing with. One of the primary challenges was ensuring that the rope itself felt like an interactive extension of the player's toolkit, rather than simply a visual line rendered between two points.

Through team discussions and prototyping, I identified that environmental interaction would be key to achieving this. I implemented a system that allowed the rope to dynamically wrap around level geometry as the player swung, preserving tension and momentum while adjusting the pivot point in real time. This significantly enhanced the sense of physicality and control.

A/B playtesting showed that players found the wrapping system more intuitive and satisfying compared to the initial fixed-anchor implementation, reinforcing the importance of environmental feedback in traversal mechanics.

Grappling While Grounded

Another key design challenge was determining how the grappling hook should behave while the player was grounded. We debated whether the hook should remain physically active, where it would apply tension and restrict movement, or whether grappling should be disabled entirely until the player was airborne.

To inform this decision, we analyzed traversal systems in games such as Just Cause and Worms, and observed player behaviour during our own playtests. Early prototypes that applied realistic tension while grounded felt mechanically consistent, but they disrupted the game's fast-paced flow and frustrated players by limiting movement at critical moments.

Given the arcade-like pacing of our game, playtesters clearly preferred responsiveness over strict physical consistency. In response, I redesigned the grounded behaviour into a hybrid solution: players could freely move without tension constraints, while still being able to fire the grappling hook and see the rope attach to surfaces. The rope remained visually present but did not apply force until the player left the ground.

This approach preserved gameplay clarity and responsiveness while maintaining visual feedback that reinforced the mechanic. The final solution struck a balance between systemic consistency and player experience, and prioritizing flow without sacrificing readability.

Undivided

Undivided is a roguelike game where you wield two forbidden masks, one of life and one of death, and shift freely between ranged and melee combat. Fight your way through a corrupted forest, collecting powerful relics that reshape your abilities with every run. Master both forms, adapt your build, and face a final boss that will test whether you were ever meant to survive this union.

This game was developed in 48 hours as part of the Global Game Jam 2026 in Singapore with the given theme of "Mask". It was also the first game jam I have participated in!

2 Developers
Feb 2026 (48 Hours)

Theme & Early Ideation

This 48-hour jam theme was "Mask".

We began with broad brainstorming rather than locking into the first idea. Our initial concepts explored themes from multiple angles, some of which included:

- A side-scroller where switching masks changed player abilities.

- A trio-based system where characters wore masks that stacked and amplified effects.

- Narrative concepts exploring identity, perspective and deception. Some also included tackling mental health issues.

- A detective story uncovering a villain represented by a recurring mask as their modus operandi

While we drawn to narrative interpretations, we ultimately chose to build a roguelike focused on mask-based combat abilities. Given the limited timeframe and our familiarity with systemic gameplay, this felt like the safest direction for delivering a complete experience. In hindsight, we prioritized familiarity over scope control, which is a valuable lesson for future jams.

Scope & Technical Direction

Choosing to make a roguelike game introduced immediate challenges. The genre is inherently content-heavy, requiring multiple enemy types, item variety, combat depth, level design and replayability.

To manage the scope, we defined a minimum playable target of one enemy type, one boss AI, two mask-based attack modes and a small pool of items with two levels. With the extra time left, we managed to expand upon this with an additional enemy type, more different items and various new level designs.

Our design goal was to ensure the mask-switching mechanic remained the core differentiator rather than building excessive content.

As development continued, the biggest challenge for this jam became the content scaling under time pressure. We had to constantly balance between the depth vs breadth of mechanics and the overall game polish vs quantity of features. Despite managing to achieve our envisioned scope, the lack of game polish made it clear that roguelikes demand significantly more content infrastructure than single-mechanic games

Key Learning Points

Scope is more important than familiarity: While we were comfortable building roguelike systems, the genre's content demands made it harder to polish individual mechanics. In future jams, I would prioritize a tighter, more mechanic-focused concept.

Modularity speeds up development: Before the jam, a tip offered by other jammers was the importance of modularity in systems, especially for projects with tight timelines. Putting it in practice, I structured the combat behaviours and enemy logic to be as modular as possible. This not only helped us iterate faster on various other types of enemies and combat, but also came with the added benefit of creating reuseable components for future projects.

Shipping over perfecting: As our first game jam, our primary goal was simply to finish and submit a playable game. Learning to make fast decisions, accept compromises, and move forward was one of the most valuable takeaways from this jam.

Slime Adventures

Slime Adventures is take on the Shoot 'em up genre where you play as a slime and have to survive against waves of enemies while making use of the slime pets available to you. Defeat as many enemies as you can, collect coins, purchase items in the shop and finally beat the boss.

This game is the result of a month long school project where the goal was to create a data-driven 2D top-down survival shooter. The focus was to develop multiple systems that will read/write from data stored in an external file.

4 Designers
Jun 2025 - Jul 2025 (1 Month)

Roles and Responsibilities

I took on the Technical Designer role for this project, where I was responsible for data serialization and related systems. Additionally, I implemented the game's menus and UI to act as the front-end for our dynamic data systems. I was also in-charge of the audio implementation.

Whilst not officially given the Product Manager role, my team relied on me to ensure that our planned features did not exceed the project's scope and were feasible given the tight deadline and team capabilities. I also helped guide any teammates who got stuck or lost throughout the development process.

Learning Takeaways

This project taught me a lot about clean data management and modular design. Using external files instead of embedded data can help streamline collaboration with a dedicated technical team in the future, especially on bigger projects.
