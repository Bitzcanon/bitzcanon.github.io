Toxic Tide

Toxic Tide is a 2D side-scrolling endless runner that has the player escaping a deadly wall of toxic gas. The player has to make use of their grappling hook to traverse through the obstacle-ridden laboratory, collect gears to upgrade their gear and survive for as long as they can.

This game is the result of a 3-month long school project where the goal was to create a 5-minute gameplay experience that emphasizes on aesthetics and engagement within a limited scope.

3 Designers | 2 Artists
Jan 2025 - Mar 2025 (3 Months)

Roles and Responsibilities

I was the Technical and Gameplay Designer for the game, where I was responsible for the grappling hook system, camera behavior, audio implementation and data management/serialization for the leaderboard and other game data. I also coordinated the art asset implementation done by our artists.

As part of a later trimester's audio-focused module, I also worked alongside another designer to compose and arrange background music for this game, where I was responsible for the Town (2nd level) and Main Menu BGM. We also sourced and tailor edited sound effects to better suit the gameplay.

As a further challenge for myself, I was also the Product Manager for this project, where I helped delegate tasks, manage deadlines and ensured that planned gameplay features were feasible within the scope and abilities of the team.

Learning Takeaways

I decided to challenge myself with implementing a satisfying grappling hook mechanic and making use of cleaner coding habits to help my teammates understand what I was writing.

This was also my first time composing / arranging music and editing sound effects, making use of techniques that we learnt during the audio module. It was a very different work process to what I was used to but something I would be happy to attempt again in future projects.

This project was my first foray into managing a team. I learnt how to cater to the needs and abilities of each team member while still meeting deadlines with high quality work. It was challenging initially as this was some members' first time developing a game, and everyone had other school modules to manage on the side.

Slime Adventures

Slime Adventures is take on the Shoot 'em up genre where you play as a slime and have to survive against waves of enemies while making use of the slime pets available to you. Defeat as many enemies as you can, collect coins, purchase items in the shop and finally beat the boss.

This game is the result of a month long school project where the goal was to create a data-driven 2D top-down survival shooter. The focus was to develop multiple systems that will read/write from data stored in an external file.

4 Designers
Jun 2025 - Jul 2025 (1 Month)

Roles and Responsibilities

I took on the Technical Designer role for this project, where I was responsible for data serialization and related systems. Additionally, I implemented the game's menus and UI to act as the front-end for our dynamic data systems. I was also in-charge of the audio implementation.

Whilst not officially given the Product Manager role, my team relied on me to ensure that our planned features did not exceed the project's scope and were feasible given the tight deadline and team capabilities. I also helped guide any teammates who got stuck or lost throughout the development process.

Learning Takeaways

This project taught me a lot about clean data management and modular design. Using external files instead of embedded data can help streamline collaboration with a dedicated technical team in the future, especially on bigger projects.

VR Wedding Proposal

This was a freelance client project where I had to develop a VR app on the Oculus Quest for the client to propose to their spouse as a surprise. It was a personal, non-commercial project that doubled as an internship to learn about VR development.

Solo
Aug 2021 - Sep 2021 (1 Month)

Roles and Responsibilities

As the solo developer in this project, I came up with the game idea proposal and developed all the necessary player interactions and level objectives.

Learning Takeaways

Being my first VR project, the whole VR development process was something completely new to me. Throughout the project, I learnt how to integrate the Oculus framework into Unity and how to use it to develop different kinds of applications. I also learnt how VR game design differed from PC/mobile games in terms of UI and UX, and how to account for the differences to ensure a smooth gameplay experience based on my knowledge of game design.

Original Brand Design

In this 3-part UI/UX school project, I had to ideate and design an original brand, along with logos, a website and mobile application. For this project, I came up with a bubble tea cat cafe as my original brand.

Solo
May 2025 - Jul 2025 (3 Months)

Part 1 - Logo Design

The logo design takes inspiration from the Chinese character for "Cat" (猫), and merging it with the form of a bubble tea cup. The colors were chosen based on color theory and psychology, and the fonts were chosen to fit the cozy vibes of the brand.

Part 2 - Website Design

The website was designed with functionality as the focus. It makes use of a clean and ordered structure, which is easy for a user to navigate around in and find the information they need.

Part 3 - Mobile App Design

The mobile app was the most challenging of the 3 to design due to space limitations and density of information. Following the website, I maintained a clean approach to the design and ensured that the user receives clear visual feedback from any action they perform.

Learning Takeaways

This project opened my eyes to the thought and care required for logo and UI design. I learnt to apply many different design principles and understood how to take advantage of them to improve the overall user experience.
